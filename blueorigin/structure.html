04 / 07DEAL ARCHITECTURE
STRUCTURE FOLLOWS STRATEGY

Not every partnership should be a JV.

The deal structure should reflect what Blue Origin actually needs from the counterparty: demand, capital, technology, operating capability, shared risk or a durable jointly owned asset.

COMMERCIAL PARTNERSHIP

Buy, sell or reserve

Use when the relationship can be governed through normal commercial obligations.

  • MSA
  • Capacity reservation
  • Volume commitment
  • Milestone pricing
CO-DEVELOPMENT

Create capability together

Use when technology or product development must happen before commercialization.

  • Development funding
  • Technical gates
  • Field-of-use IP
  • Commercialization rights
PARTNER-LINKED FINANCIAL

Trade upside for commitment

Use warrants, options or credits when an anchor commitment materially de-risks the program.

  • Non-cancellable deposits
  • Warrant vesting
  • Volume thresholds
  • Performance gates
JV / SPV

Share an asset and economics

Use when both parties contribute meaningful capital, IP or operating expertise to a separate business.

  • Capital contribution
  • Governance
  • IP ownership
  • Exit / transfer rights

TERM-SHEET DISCIPLINE

Financial incentives should vest only when strategic value appears.

The notes propose milestone-linked warrants and options. I would keep the principle but negotiate the exact economics only after valuation, tax, accounting and governance review.

COMMITMENT

What is the partner guaranteeing?

Deposits, minimum volume, CapEx, technical resources, distribution or operating capacity.

VESTING

When is upside earned?

Contract execution, technical qualification, first successful mission, cumulative volume or revenue milestones.

IP

Who owns what?

Protect background IP. Define foreground IP, field-of-use rights, licensing and post-termination rights.

GOVERNANCE

Who controls the decision?

Board rights, reserved matters, deadlock, funding calls, exit, change of control and termination.
