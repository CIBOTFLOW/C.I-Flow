COMMERCIAL OPERATING MODEL

Different role. Same underlying logic.

Business Development, Sales and Partnerships have different ownership boundaries, but I approach all three as connected information and execution systems.

Application map

Define the real applications, buyer problems, business metrics, differentiators, implementation requirements and places the product should not be forced.

ICP + account intelligence

Segment by industry and use case, then enrich with firmographics, installed technology, initiatives, triggers and economic context. The score has to explain itself.

People + relationship map

Map economic buyers, decision makers, champions, technical evaluators, procurement, blockers, partners and warm paths—not just contact titles.

Opportunity hypothesis

Connect a specific business problem to the application, value model, implementation path and expansion logic. No account gets pursued merely because it is large.

Motion design

Choose direct, partner-led, co-sell, marketplace, event, executive, technical or multi-threaded motions based on how the account is actually likely to buy.

Qualification + progression

Track consequence, access, value, technical feasibility, budget path, competition, next step and mutual commitments. The system should surface what is missing.

Partner economics

A partnership must create value for the partner: implementation revenue, cloud consumption, account access, differentiated services, retention or expansion. “Strategic” without an operating motion is not enough.

Win/loss learning

Closed-won gets the highest evidentiary weight, open pipeline informs current patterns, and closed-lost explains where positioning, fit, access or execution breaks down.

Next best action

The output is not another dashboard. It is a prioritized action: who to contact, why, with what hypothesis, through which route and what evidence would change the plan.

ROLE-SPECIFIC OUTPUT

BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT
