HOW I SELL

I do not start with the pitch. I start with the account.

Complex enterprise deals move when the seller understands the operating problem, the decision process, the economic consequence, the implementation path and the evidence still missing.

01ProblemIs there a workflow worth changing?
02ConsequenceWhat happens if nothing changes?
03PeopleWho funds, decides, uses and can block?
04ProcessHow will the organization reach a decision?
05ProofWhat evidence moves this into production?
Frameworks help me organize evidence. They do not replace judgment.
01

Reconstruct the market and choose the entry point.

I define the ICP, urgency, competitive context and entry wedge. A crowded category does not mean every buyer or workflow is equally saturated.

02

Prioritize accounts by consequence, not list position.

I look at fit, timing, signals, organizational complexity, reachable stakeholders and potential value. High-value human work should go to the accounts where there is a reason to act now.

03

Build a value hypothesis before outreach.

I want a plausible view of the workflow, cost of the current state, measurable value and why this company specifically may care. Outreach then begins with a reason, not a template.

04

Map the organization as a decision system.

Economic buyer, champion, decision makers, technical stakeholders, users, procurement, security and likely blockers. Enterprise selling is consensus building, not persuasion of one person.

05

Use discovery to create value.

I use BANT where it is useful to establish budget, authority, need and timing, but discovery must go further: current workflow, consequence, constraints, success metrics, political reality and what would make doing nothing rational.

06

Use MEDDPICC to expose deal risk.

Metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, paper process, pain, champion and competition become a gap map. The framework is useful when it tells me what evidence is missing, not when it simply fills CRM fields.

07

Design pilots backward from production.

A pilot should answer a buying question. I want success criteria, business owner, technical owner, timeline, production path and commercial consequence agreed before the experiment starts.

08

Negotiate around value and alternatives.

I separate price from value, understand what procurement actually controls, preserve mutual commitments and avoid discounting simply because the deal is late.

09

Protect the handoff.

The implementation team should inherit the promised outcomes, stakeholder map, risks, success metrics and expansion thesis, not only a closed-won record.

10

Expand through adjacent workflows.

Expansion is often workflow-based, not seat-based. One successful use case can reveal the next: account intelligence, proposal creation, customer success, renewal support, service or operations.

COMMERCIAL OPERATING PRINCIPLES

What I refuse to confuse.

These distinctions keep deals grounded in evidence rather than activity.

INTEREST ≠ DEAL

Enthusiasm is not evidence.

A deal exists when the decision process, business value and path to implementation are becoming real.

PILOT ≠ PROGRESS

Experiments can hide indecision.

A pilot without a production decision is often a delay mechanism.

ACTIVITY ≠ CONTROL

More touches do not fix a weak account.

Better account understanding, multi-threading and the right next action do.
