THE MOTION

From named account to repeatable revenue.

Frameworks help organize evidence. They do not replace commercial judgment.

01

Choose the lane and account universe.

Start with the vendor's strongest use case, buyer and industry fit. Then narrow the European AI 1000 into the companies where the problem and budget are most plausible.

02

Build the account hypothesis.

Research the workflow, organization, likely economic consequence, technology context and why the timing might matter before outreach begins.

03

Create contact, not just activity.

Call, email and use LinkedIn with a company-specific reason to engage. The goal is a useful commercial conversation, not an activity count.

04

Use discovery to find the real buying problem.

Understand the current workflow, cost, capacity, risk, timing, stakeholders and why doing nothing might still be rational.

05

Map the organization as a decision system.

Economic buyer, champion, users, IT, security, procurement, finance and blockers. Complex AI sales require consensus, not one enthusiastic contact.

06

Decide who should close.

If the vendor has a strong AE and technical team, hand off with context. If shared ownership improves the odds, co-sell. If the vendor needs European coverage, keep Connor in the lead through signature.

07

Design proof around a production decision.

Pilots need success criteria, owners, security path, decision date and commercial consequence before they begin.

08

Expand across workflows and lanes.

A successful customer-service deployment may reveal workflow, procurement or security opportunities. The value of the territory compounds when the same relationship can support more than one product.
