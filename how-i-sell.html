HOW I SELL

I do not start with the pitch. I start with reality.

Complex enterprise deals move when the seller understands the account better: the operating problem, the decision system, the economic consequence, the implementation path and the evidence still missing.

01

Reconstruct the market and choose the battlefield.

I define the ICP, urgency, competitive context and entry wedge. A crowded category does not mean the entire market is saturated; the right vertical, workflow, buyer or failure mode can create a much smaller competitive field.

02

Prioritize accounts by consequence, not list position.

I look at fit, timing, signals, organizational complexity, reachable stakeholders and potential value. High-value human work should go to the accounts where there is a reason to act now.

03

Build a value hypothesis before outreach.

I want a plausible view of the workflow, cost of the current state, measurable value and why this company specifically may care. Outreach then begins with a reason, not a template.

04

Map the organization as a decision system.

Economic buyer, champion, decision makers, technical stakeholders, users, procurement, security and likely blockers. Enterprise selling is construction of consensus, not persuasion of one person.

05

Use discovery to create value.

I use BANT where it is useful to establish budget, authority, need and timing, but discovery must go further: current workflow, consequence, constraints, success metrics, political reality and what would make doing nothing rational.

06

Use MEDDPICC to expose deal risk.

Metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, paper process, pain, champion and competition become a gap map. The framework is useful when it tells us what evidence is missing—not when it becomes CRM theater.

07

Design pilots backward from production.

A pilot should answer a buying question. I want success criteria, business owner, technical owner, timeline, production path and commercial consequence agreed before the experiment becomes free consulting.

08

Negotiate around value and alternatives.

I separate price from value, understand what procurement actually controls, preserve mutual commitments and avoid discounting simply because the deal is late.

09

Protect the handoff.

The implementation team should inherit the promised outcomes, stakeholder map, risks, success metrics and expansion thesis—not a closed-won record with no context.

10

Expand through adjacent workflows.

AI land-and-expand is increasingly architectural. One successful workflow can reveal the next: account intelligence → proposal creation → customer success → renewal intelligence → support → operations.

COMMERCIAL OPERATING PRINCIPLES

What I refuse to confuse.

These distinctions keep deals grounded in evidence rather than activity.

INTEREST ≠ DEAL

Enthusiasm is not evidence.

A deal exists when the decision process, business value and path to implementation are becoming real.

PILOT ≠ PROGRESS

Experiments can hide indecision.

A pilot without a production decision is often a delay mechanism.

ACTIVITY ≠ CONTROL

More touches do not fix a weak account.

Better account understanding, multi-threading and the right next action do.
