MY COMMERCIAL THESIS

AI capability is abundant. Commercial proof and distribution are scarce.

The hard part is increasingly not showing that AI can do something. It is proving that the workflow should change, that the economics matter, that the organization can adopt it safely, and that the right direct or partner-led route can get it into production and expansion.

01

Choose the battlefield.

I separate category saturation from actual market saturation and look for a wedge where incumbent strengths matter less.

02

Define fundable value.

I translate “AI productivity” into capacity, conversion, velocity, risk, management or option value a buyer can actually defend.

03

Build leverage.

I evaluate whether the fastest route is direct selling, technology integration, co-sell, channel, reseller, marketplace or strategic alliance.

04

Architect expansion.

I treat land-and-expand as workflow and relationship architecture: each successful deployment should reveal the next valuable operating problem.

Open PartnershipsOpen the Deal Lab
“Sometimes the best AI sales answer is: you do not need AI yet.”

If the real problem is broken CRM data, incoherent process, conflicting definitions, permissions, or basic automation, forcing AI into the conversation destroys trust. I want the outcome to be right before I want the technology to be AI.

“A partnership should change behavior, not just create an announcement.”

I want a clear joint customer problem, mutual economics, field ownership, technical/commercial responsibilities and a scorecard that tells both sides whether to scale the relationship.
