MY COMMERCIAL THESIS

AI capability is abundant. Commercial proof is scarce.

The hard part is increasingly not showing that AI can do something. It is proving that the workflow should change, that the economics matter, that the organization can adopt it safely, and that a successful deployment has somewhere to expand.

01

Choose the battlefield.

I separate category saturation from actual market saturation and look for a wedge where incumbent strengths matter less.

02

Define fundable value.

I translate “AI productivity” into capacity, conversion, velocity, risk, management or option value the buyer can actually defend.

03

Design the decision.

I map the people, evidence, security, implementation path and success criteria needed to turn interest into organizational consensus.

04

Architect expansion.

I treat land-and-expand as workflow architecture: each successful deployment should reveal the next valuable operating problem.

“Sometimes the best AI sales answer is: you do not need AI yet.”

If the real problem is broken CRM data, incoherent process, conflicting definitions, permissions, or basic automation, forcing AI into the conversation destroys trust. I want the outcome to be right before I want the technology to be AI.

“The strongest AI BDM sells the outcome, not AI usage.”

That means understanding the customer’s operating model, implementation cost, adoption constraints, economics and political reality—not just product features and objection handling.
