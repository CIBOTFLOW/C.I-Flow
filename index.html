MY COMMERCIAL THESIS

Capability is easier to access. Clear business value and execution are not.

Most enterprise buyers are not deciding whether AI is interesting. They are deciding whether a workflow is worth changing, whether the economics hold, whether the organization can implement it safely, and whether a direct or partner route improves the odds of adoption.

01

Choose the market.

Find the buyer, workflow and timing where the problem is important enough to create action.

02

Build the business case.

Translate product capability into capacity, conversion, velocity, risk, cost or another value the buyer can defend.

03

Choose the route.

Decide whether the best path is direct selling, integration, co-sell, channel, reseller, marketplace or alliance.

04

Plan the next step.

Design the first deployment so success can lead naturally into the next workflow, team or partner motion.

The route can change. The commercial question does not: is there enough value, evidence and organizational support to change behavior?
“Sometimes the right sales answer is: you do not need AI yet.”

If the real problem is broken CRM data, an incoherent process, conflicting definitions, permissions or basic automation, forcing AI into the conversation destroys trust. I would rather diagnose the problem correctly.

“A partnership should change behavior, not just create an announcement.”

I want a clear joint customer problem, mutual economics, field ownership, technical and commercial responsibilities, and a scorecard that tells both sides whether to scale the relationship.
