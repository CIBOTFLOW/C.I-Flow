THE BOARD MEETING SHOULD CONFIRM THE DECISION

Do the hard selling before the vote.

HOA sales cycles naturally stall because boards may meet once a month. The answer is not pressure. It is structured pre-alignment, useful work between meetings, and a transition plan that removes the cost of saying yes.

TIMELINE COMPRESSION

Three ways to move without skipping governance.

The goal is to shorten dead time while respecting the board's actual approval process and governing documents.

01 · PRE-ALIGNPresident + Treasurer first

Resolve economics, service expectations and transition concerns before the full board presentation.

02 · WORK BETWEEN MEETINGS15-20 minute executive session

Review the audit, answer questions and prepare the formal decision rather than waiting four weeks to continue discovery.

03 · REMOVE SWITCHING FEAROne-page transition MAP

Show exactly who owns banking, records, vendors, residents, communications and the incumbent handoff.

MUTUAL ACTION PLAN

Make the path from audit to signed agreement visible.

Every late-stage opportunity should have a decision map with board dates, owners, required documents and transition dependencies.

DISCOVERY

Operational audit complete

Current-state gaps, incumbent contract and decision criteria documented.

ALIGNMENT

President / Treasurer working session

Economics, service model, transition plan and open objections resolved.

DECISION

Board presentation + vote

Formal recommendation presented with a clear implementation path.

SIGNATURE

Agreement + transition launch

Operations receives the community, documents, owners and day-one timeline.

30-DAY TRANSITION

The handoff is part of the sale.

The exact process belongs to Assembly Operations. BD should understand it well enough to set accurate expectations and sell confidence, then stop making promises outside the operating playbook.

1Contract + notice

Effective date, incumbent obligations and communication plan confirmed.

2Banking + finance

Operating / reserve accounts, balances, dues and AP information transferred.

3Records + governance

CC&Rs, bylaws, minutes, rosters, policies and active issues organized.

4Vendors + maintenance

Contracts, contacts, recurring work, open tickets and capital projects mapped.

5Resident launch

Welcome, access, payment instructions, support routes and expectations communicated.

BD / Ops rule: never close a deal by creating an onboarding problem. CRM should capture transition complexity before signature so Operations knows what is coming.
BOARD PRESENTATION

One meeting, five decisions.

The deck should be short enough to discuss, not present at people.

1What is broken?

Use their audit findings, not generic industry pain.

2What changes?

Show management, platform and Atlas in the context of those problems.

3What is the value?

Fees, board time, vendor workflow and risk reduction.

4What is the risk?

Address data, service model, contract and switching concerns directly.

5How do we switch?

Show the transition MAP and owners.

6What happens tonight?

Vote, next action, documentation and signature path.
