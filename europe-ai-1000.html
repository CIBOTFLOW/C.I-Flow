HOW WE SELL THE 1,000

One account plan. Multiple channels. Multiple buying centers.

The 1,000 accounts are not a list to blast. Each priority account gets a commercial hypothesis, a mapped buying committee and coordinated outreach across the channels most likely to create a real conversation.

01

LinkedIn

Map the organization, follow role changes and initiatives, warm the account and reach specific people with context. It supports the account plan; it is not a mass-DM channel.

02

Phone

The fastest way to test the hypothesis, qualify timing and learn how the organization actually works. Calls also help us move laterally when the first contact is not the buyer.

03

Email

Short, account-specific outreach built around a trigger, a business hypothesis and one useful next step. We localize language and examples when the market or buyer benefits from it.

04

Newsletter

Permission-based, multilingual nurture for active prospects, past conversations and market relationships. Different editions can speak to finance, operations, technology and security audiences.

05

Articles & briefs

Useful point-of-view content for specific sectors and workflows: benchmarks, use cases, buying guides and implementation lessons that can support outreach and follow-up.

06

Video

Short account videos for high-priority pursuits plus reusable industry explainers. A 60-second explanation of the problem and business case often travels inside the account better than another long email.

07

Partner introductions

Use customers, consultants, integrators, technology partners and industry relationships to create trusted entry points where cold outreach would be slower.

08

Executive roundtables

Small webinars or roundtables around one operating problem. Bring together a handful of relevant leaders, create useful discussion and turn one event into several account conversations.

ORGANIZATIONAL MAPPING

Sell to the decision system, not one contact.

Enterprise AI deals usually cross business, technical and control functions. We map influence, ownership and approval early so a strong first meeting becomes a coordinated buying process instead of a stalled champion.

ECONOMIC BUYER

C-Level & budget owners

CEO, COO, CFO, CIO, CISO, Chief Customer Officer or another executive who can fund the change and defend the business case.

BUSINESS OWNER

Operational leaders

VP/Head of Customer Service, Procurement, Finance Operations, Transformation, Shared Services or the function that owns the workflow.

TECHNICAL LEADERS

Architecture, data & AI

CTO, Head of AI, Data/ML leaders, Enterprise Architects, IT leaders and platform owners who determine technical fit and implementation reality.

CHAMPIONS & INFLUENCERS

People who create momentum

Directors, managers, innovation teams, digital leaders and domain experts who understand the pain, shape requirements and carry the idea internally.

RISK & APPROVAL

Security, legal & procurement

Security, privacy, compliance, legal, procurement and finance stakeholders who can slow or stop the deal if they appear too late.

USERS & IMPLEMENTERS

The people who make it work

Frontline teams, admins, operations, systems owners and implementation leads who validate whether the proposed workflow will survive production.

Mapping rule

Before a serious proposal, aim to understand the economic buyer, operating owner, technical decision maker, risk path and at least one credible internal champion. Titles vary by company; the roles in the decision do not.
