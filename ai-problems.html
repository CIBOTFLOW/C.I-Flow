ENTERPRISE AGENT COMMERCIAL PROBLEMS

The hard part is turning capability into a business decision.

Enterprise agents can demo beautifully and still die in pilot. Production requires a workflow worth redesigning, measurable value, trusted context, system access, governance, stakeholder alignment and a credible path from human-supervised execution toward the right level of autonomy.

Differentiation gets harder

What I see

Agent platforms increasingly overlap in demos. A generic claim that one model or agent is 'smarter' is easy for a CIO to dismiss.

How I respond

I move the discussion to the workflow, architecture layer, context source, action path, controls, reliability and production outcome that matter in that account.

Weak value case

What I see

The demo is impressive, but the CFO hears “productivity” and cannot connect it to a defendable business case.

How I respond

I quantify the value the buyer actually funds: capacity, conversion, velocity, risk, management visibility, cost or strategic option value.

Pilot without a decision

What I see

A 90-day pilot becomes an experiment with no production decision, no stakeholder plan and no consequence if the metrics are met.

How I respond

I work backward from production before the pilot begins: success metrics, economic buyer, technical approval, implementation owner, decision date, commercial path and expansion criteria.

Implementation drag

What I see

The buyer likes the product, but security, data, IT bandwidth, CRM quality or another internal initiative becomes the real competitor.

How I respond

I surface those dependencies during discovery, reduce the first deployment to a manageable operating change, identify owners early and build a mutual path through the work.

Risk and trust

What I see

The more a system can do, including writing to systems, contacting customers or changing records, the more risk it creates for security and legal stakeholders.

How I respond

I bring controls, permissions, logging, human review, failure handling and security stakeholders into the plan instead of treating them as late-stage objections.

Buyer consensus

What I see

The business sponsor wants speed, IT wants control, finance wants proof, users fear disruption and procurement wants leverage.

How I respond

I treat the account as a decision system. One champion is not enough. I want enough aligned evidence for the organization to say yes.

Renewal proof

What I see

Initial enthusiasm can win a first contract. Renewal forces the buyer to ask what actually changed.

How I respond

I build outcome evidence during the contract, not at renewal: baseline, adoption, workflow completion, business consequence and unresolved opportunity.

Expansion stalls

What I see

A successful agentic workflow stays isolated because nobody maps the adjacent workflows, architecture dependencies, stakeholders and stack layers.

How I respond

I look for the next operating problem the first workflow reveals. Expansion should feel like solving the next logical problem, not simply selling more seats.

What is actually blocking the deal?

Select the friction. The next move should change with the problem.

Next move

Prove the business case

Start with the current workflow, baseline the cost or capacity constraint, and quantify the value type the buyer actually funds. Bring finance into the definition before the pilot, not after it.

VALUE ARCHITECTURE

ROI is not one thing.

The useful question is not “Can I build an ROI calculator?” It is which form of value matters enough to this buyer that they will fund the change.

Efficiency

Less human time required per task or workflow.

Capacity

The same team can manage more accounts, customers, cases or work.

Conversion

Better decisions or execution improve win, activation or resolution rates.

Velocity

Cycle time falls and value is realized sooner.

Risk

Fewer errors, missed accounts, compliance events or uncontrolled actions.

Option value

The organization can attempt a workflow or market motion that was previously uneconomic.

MY RULE

If an agent should not own the workflow—or should remain tightly human-supervised—I would rather say that early. Trust is more valuable than forcing the wrong technology into a pipeline.
